Fifteen Years in the Game

I began playing golf at the age of seven. My father, who has always loved the game, first introduced me to it with a simple belief: golf teaches persistence, reflection, and the discipline to keep improving after failure.

2020 Chinese Men’s National Team 2023 Iowa State Men’s Golf NCAA Division I Student-Athlete

Golf has stayed with me for more than fifteen years, from elementary school in China to NCAA Division I competition in the United States. Along the way, I competed at increasingly higher levels, joined the Chinese Men’s National Team in 2020, and came to Iowa State University in 2023 to compete in NCAA Division I golf.

Throughout this journey, I have tried to honor the word student in student-athlete. Academics have always come first, while competitive golf has taught me how to manage time, work patiently toward long-term goals, and remain curious about why things succeed or fail.

A Practice of Revising

Competitive golf has given me thousands of opportunities to make a decision, observe an outcome, and revise my approach.

A bad round cannot be undone. A good round guarantees nothing about tomorrow. Progress comes from extracting useful information from both, then returning to the work.

The same mindset shapes the way I approach mathematics: stay patient with difficult problems, question failed approaches, use what I have learned, and keep going long enough to understand the structure underneath.

Where it began

Golf began as something I shared with my father.

Fifteen years later, it remains one of the most important parts of my life. I expect it always will be.
