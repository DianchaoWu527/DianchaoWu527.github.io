Research 01

Berry–Esseen Bounds for Periodic Langevin Dynamics

Research poster

O. Smith, A. Valse, D. Wu (alphabetical). Manuscript coming soon. Mentor: Professor David P. Herzog.

This project establishes Berry–Esseen bounds for the Gaussian approximation of unwrapped displacement in periodic Langevin dynamics. My starting question for Proposition 11.1 was how to control the Berry–Esseen constant in the main theorem as the friction parameter \(\gamma\) tends to zero.

NSF-funded research · Award DMS-2246491

View research poster → Download poster PDF

My contributions

  • Formulated the Berry–Esseen framework for the unwrapped displacement and proved the overdamped results.
  • Proved the main underdamped theorem and supporting propositions via a direct Markov-additive treatment of the displacement; verification of Assumption (A2) was carried out by O. Smith.
  • Proved in Proposition 11.1 that, for one-dimensional periodic underdamped dynamics started from equilibrium and sufficiently small positive \(\gamma\), the Berry–Esseen constant C\(\gamma\) can be chosen with \(C_{\gamma} \le C\gamma^{-1/2}\), where C is independent of \(\gamma\).
  • Simulated overdamped and underdamped transport, measured the friction-dependent constants, and compared them with the averaged energy dynamics.

The joint manuscript also includes additional numerical analysis by A. Valse.

Core contribution · Proposition 11.1

Controlling the Berry–Esseen constant in the low-friction regime

For the one-dimensional periodic underdamped system started from equilibrium, I proved that for sufficiently small positive \(\gamma\) the Berry–Esseen constant can be chosen so that

\[ C_{\gamma} \le C\gamma^{-1/2}. \]

Here C is independent of \(\gamma\) and observation time.

Equivalently, for \(T \ge 1\) the Kolmogorov-distance estimate reads

\[ d_K\!\left(\mathcal{L}(Y_T^{\gamma}),\,\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\gamma}^{2})\right) \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{\gamma T}}. \]

The form \(C/\sqrt{\gamma T}\) highlights \(\gamma T\) as the combined parameter in the error bound; the main contribution is the quantified dependence of the Berry–Esseen constant on \(\gamma\).

Proof architecture
  1. 01
    Sample at the relaxation scale. Partition the process into blocks of length comparable to \(\gamma^{-1}\) and form a discrete skeleton chain.
  2. 02
    Recover uniform mixing. Rescale the continuous-time semigroup estimate so the sampled chain has a spectral gap uniform in small \(\gamma\).
  3. 03
    Control moments and variance. Establish a uniform fourth-moment bound for block-averaged velocity and use a one-dimensional diffusion lower bound to prevent variance degeneration.
  4. 04
    Apply a uniform Berry–Esseen theorem. The Markov-additive formulation then yields the \(C/\sqrt{\gamma T}\) Kolmogorov-distance estimate.

Research 02

Random Prepayment Sensitivity in Nonlinear MBS Pricing

Working manuscript

I study how small random changes in prepayment intensity affect mortgage-backed security prices. The current manuscript focuses on the Papi–Briani model at \(p=2\), using a probabilistic representation to identify the linear price response, control its approximation error, and quantify the resulting mean and variance.

How do random prepayment environments change the pricing solution, its mean, and its variance?

Current manuscript · random coefficients at \(p=2\)

The model tracks a financial factor and cumulative prepayment on \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Prepayment intensity is perturbed as \(\eta^{\varepsilon}=\eta^0(1+\varepsilon\zeta)\), where \(\zeta\) has continuous paths and a deterministic uniform bound. Each coefficient scenario defines a degenerate nonlinear pricing PDE. The environmental noise selects the scenario; the Brownian motion in the BSDE is an auxiliary tool for representing its solution.

  • Use Papi’s viscosity theory for well-posedness and price bounds, then remove the quadratic gradient term through a change of unknown to obtain a Lipschitz BSDE representation.
  • Establish a bounded linear response operator and a uniform second-order remainder, giving Fréchet differentiability in the supremum norm.
  • Derive mean-square sensitivity, mean-price bias, and Hilbert-space variance expansions; express the leading variance through the covariance of the input noise.
  • Work with continuous bounded perturbations, without requiring their time derivatives or classical spatial derivatives of the price.

Manuscript results · Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2

Linear price response with a quadratic remainder

\[ P^{\varepsilon}=P^0+\varepsilon Y+R^{\varepsilon},\qquad Y=L\zeta. \]

Here \(P^{\varepsilon}\) is the full security value and \(L\) is a deterministic bounded linear operator obtained from the forward–backward variational system. Under the manuscript’s assumptions, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), almost surely,

\[ \lVert R^{\varepsilon}\rVert_{\infty}\le C\varepsilon^2\lVert\zeta\rVert_{\infty}^2. \]

The bound is uniform in the state and time, and also gives an \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) remainder in \(L^2(\Omega;C([0,T];H))\), where \(H=L^2(\mathbb{R}^2,\nu)\) for a probability measure \(\nu\). For centered perturbations, the mean-price bias is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), and uniformly in \(t\),

\[ \operatorname{Var}_H(P^{\varepsilon}(t))=\varepsilon^2\mathbb{E}\lVert Y(t)\rVert_H^2+O(|\varepsilon|^3). \]
Proof approach · from the pricing PDE to its response

Viscosity well-posedness supplies a bounded price range. A power transform cancels the quadratic gradient term, and the transformed solution is identified with the value function of a Lipschitz BSDE. Linearizing the forward prepayment dynamics and the backward equation, then controlling the Taylor remainders and undoing the transform, yields the uniform sensitivity estimate. A deterministic response kernel links the leading price variance to the covariance of the prepayment perturbation.

Planned next stage · Spring 2027

SPDE extension with additive noise

In Spring 2027, I plan to introduce additive stochastic forcing directly into the MBS evolution equation. The proposed form is schematic:

\[ du_t^{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{F}(t,u_t^{\varepsilon})\,dt+\varepsilon G(t)\,dW_t. \]

Here \(\mathcal{F}\) denotes the chosen MBS PDE operator, \(W\) is a Wiener process, and the noise coefficient \(G(t)\) is independent of the solution. This adds a stochastic integral to the evolution itself. The analytical setting and noise regularity will be specified as part of this planned work.
