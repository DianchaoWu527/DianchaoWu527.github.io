Berry–Esseen Bounds for Periodic Langevin Dynamics

This project establishes Berry–Esseen bounds for the Gaussian approximation of unwrapped displacement in periodic Langevin dynamics. My starting question for Proposition 11.1 was how to control the Berry–Esseen constant in the main theorem as the friction parameter \(\gamma\) tends to zero.

Core contribution · Proposition 11.1

Controlling the Berry–Esseen constant in the low-friction regime

For the one-dimensional periodic underdamped system started from equilibrium, I proved that for sufficiently small positive \(\gamma\) the Berry–Esseen constant can be chosen so that

\[ C_{\gamma} \le C\gamma^{-1/2}. \]

Here C is independent of \(\gamma\). This controls an admissible Berry–Esseen constant; it does not identify the optimal constant.

Equivalently, for \(T \ge 1\) the Kolmogorov-distance estimate reads

\[ d_K\!\left(\mathcal{L}(Y_T^{\gamma}),\,\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\gamma}^{2})\right) \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{\gamma T}}. \]

The form \(C/\sqrt{\gamma T}\) highlights \(\gamma T\) as the combined parameter in the error bound; the main contribution is the quantified dependence of the Berry–Esseen constant on \(\gamma\).

Proof architecture
  1. 01
    Sample at the relaxation scale. Partition the process into blocks of length comparable to \(\gamma^{-1}\) and form a discrete skeleton chain.
  2. 02
    Recover uniform mixing. Rescale the continuous-time semigroup estimate so the sampled chain has a spectral gap uniform in small \(\gamma\).
  3. 03
    Control moments and variance. Establish a uniform fourth-moment bound for block-averaged velocity and use a one-dimensional diffusion lower bound to prevent variance degeneration.
  4. 04
    Apply a uniform Berry–Esseen theorem. The Markov-additive formulation then yields the \(C/\sqrt{\gamma T}\) Kolmogorov-distance estimate.

Research 02

Stochastic Sensitivity and Model Risk in Nonlinear MBS Pricing Models

In progress

I study how uncertainty in prepayment and model coefficients propagates through nonlinear PDE models for mortgage-backed securities. Current work develops pathwise estimates and small-noise sensitivity analysis for a reduced MBS equation, alongside ongoing extensions involving p-Laplacian diffusion.

How do random prepayment environments change the pricing solution, its mean, and its variance?

Current stochastic analysis

The current working analysis treats bounded \(C^1\) random perturbations of the prepayment environment in the periodic reduced semilinear model, under a positive baseline forcing condition. Randomness enters through the coefficients; each realization is analyzed as a parabolic PDE.

  • Developed pathwise well-posedness and uniform solution bounds, yielding finite-order price moment estimates.
  • Obtained mean-square stability estimates and derived an explicit linear equation for the first-order price sensitivity.
  • Developed a second-order remainder estimate and, for centered perturbations, expansions for the mean price and Hilbert-space variance.
  • Used Fourier spectral simulations to examine first- and second-order perturbation scaling.

Working analysis · reduced semilinear model

A first-order description of prepayment uncertainty

\[ u^{\varepsilon}=u^0+\varepsilon U+R^{\varepsilon}. \]

Here \(U\) solves the linearized sensitivity equation. Under the working assumptions, the remainder in \(X=L^2(\Omega;C([0,T];L^2))\) satisfies

\[ \lVert R^{\varepsilon}\rVert_X \le C\varepsilon^2. \]

For centered perturbations, the mean-price bias is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) and the Hilbert-space variance satisfies

\[ \operatorname{Var}_{L^2}(u^{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon^2\,\mathbb{E}\lVert U\rVert_{L^2}^{2}+O(|\varepsilon|^3). \]

Extend the stability and sensitivity framework to fixed-p nonlinear diffusion, where degeneracy or singularity near zero gradients makes differentiability a central question.

02

Study joint prepayment and interest-rate uncertainty, including the role of covariance in model-risk decomposition.

03

Future SPDE direction

A planned extension introduces stochastic forcing directly into the evolution equation and studies stochastic moment bounds and the zero-noise limit.

Model interpretation and market evidence

The project began with stochastic perturbations and p-Laplacian modifications of an existing pricing PDE. Initial backtests using real MBS transaction data have not established a stable predictive improvement. Current analytical results concern uncertainty propagation under specified model assumptions; the financial interpretation and empirical validation of nonlinear diffusion remain ongoing questions.
